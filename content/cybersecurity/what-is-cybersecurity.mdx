Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, data, and digital services from unauthorized access, misuse, disruption, or destruction. It spans technology, processes, and people.

Organizations invest in cybersecurity because modern systems handle sensitive information such as customer records, financial data, and operational controls. When those systems fail, the impact can include financial loss, legal exposure, and loss of public trust.

## The CIA Triad

Most introductory security frameworks begin with three core goals:

- **Confidentiality** — only authorized users can access information.
- **Integrity** — information remains accurate and unaltered unless changed by an authorized process.
- **Availability** — systems and data remain accessible to authorized users when needed.

Security controls are often evaluated by how well they support one or more of these properties.

## Common Threat Categories

Security teams typically organize risks into recognizable categories:

1. **Malware** — malicious software such as ransomware or spyware.
2. **Phishing** — deceptive messages designed to trick users into revealing credentials.
3. **Misconfiguration** — insecure defaults in cloud services, applications, or devices.
4. **Credential abuse** — stolen or weak passwords used to access accounts.
5. **Insider risk** — accidental or intentional misuse by people with legitimate access.

Understanding the threat category helps teams choose the right control. For example, phishing is mitigated with security awareness training, email filtering, and multi-factor authentication.

## Defense in Depth

No single control stops every attack. Effective programs use **defense in depth**: multiple layers such as network segmentation, endpoint protection, logging, patching, and incident response.

Patch management is a basic but critical control. Unpatched software remains one of the most common root causes of compromise.

## Where to Go Next

If you are new to the field, focus on fundamentals before advanced tooling:

- Learn how operating systems, networks, and identity systems work.
- Practice secure habits such as password managers and MFA.
- Study how incidents are detected through logs and alerts.

Cybersecurity is a continuous discipline, not a one-time project. The goal is to reduce risk to an acceptable level while keeping systems usable for the people who depend on them.
